01 / THE TRIGGER

A cascading hazard begins above the border.

At about 9:00 a.m., water in the Bhote Koshi basin began rising rapidly. Scientists are investigating whether an ice-rock avalanche entered Lhende Khola, temporarily blocked the channel, then released a surge of water, sediment and boulders.

The trigger remains under investigation. The path downstream is not.

SOURCE · ICIMOD, 26 AUG 2026

02 / BHOTE KOSHI

The surge crossed into Nepal at Rasuwagadhi.

Compressed by a steep, narrow valley, the flood tore through the border corridor and moved toward Syabrubesi. Roads, bridges and monitoring stations were damaged or swept away.

9 mreported rise at Galchhi in 30 minutes

03 / TRISHULI

The hazard widened as the rivers joined.

Below Syabrubesi, the Bhote Koshi becomes the Trishuli. The flood moved south through Rasuwa and Nuwakot toward Dhading—turning a mountain disaster into a long river-corridor emergency.

Five districts were affected: Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha and Tanahun.

04 / SCHOOLS

Nineteen schools are reported damaged.

UNICEF’s 31 August situation report gives the first corridor-wide education assessment. The count remains preliminary as field teams reach isolated communities.

05 / WHAT THE SATELLITE SEES

Every point needs verification.

Sentinel-2 can reveal channel widening, debris, sediment and major building loss at 10-metre resolution. It cannot, by itself, establish whether a school is safe to occupy.

The image viewer selects the lowest-cloud scene available before and after 26 August. Cloud, shadow, terrain and the satellite’s revisit timing can obscure damage; field assessment remains the authority.
